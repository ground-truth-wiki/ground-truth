> [!tldr]
> Biology gave deep learning its clearest scientific win: predicting the three-dimensional structure of a protein from its amino-acid sequence, a problem that had resisted fifty years of work, and then running the same models backwards to design proteins that do not exist in nature. The field now spans structure prediction, protein language models, generative design and genome-scale sequence models, with a parallel policy debate about dual use. The one thing to remember: a prediction is a hypothesis, and in biology the experiment, not the model's own score, is what settles it.

## Key ideas

- **The folding problem was measured by a blind benchmark long before it was solved.** A protein is a chain of amino acids that folds into a specific shape, and the shape determines what it does. Since 1994 the Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction (CASP) has run roughly every two years, testing methods through blind prediction ([Protein Structure Prediction Center](https://predictioncenter.org/)): predictors get sequences whose experimental structures exist but are not yet published, so no one can look up the answer, and the most recent round closed its prediction season in September 2026 ([CASP17](https://predictioncenter.org/casp17/index.cgi)). At CASP14 in 2020, AlphaFold 2 reached a median backbone error of 0.96 Å against 2.8 Å for the next best method, which its authors described as competitive with experimental structures in a majority of cases (Jumper et al., [“Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8371605/) (2021)). The 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went one half to David Baker “for computational protein design” and one half jointly to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper “for protein structure prediction” ([The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, October 2024](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/press-release/)).
- **A public database turned structure prediction into infrastructure.** Google DeepMind and EMBL-EBI launched the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database in July 2021 with over 350,000 structures and expanded it to over 200 million in July 2022 ([Google DeepMind, AlphaFold](https://deepmind.google/science/alphafold/)), which the Nobel committee described as the structure of virtually all the 200 million proteins researchers have identified, used by more than two million people from 190 countries ([The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, October 2024](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/press-release/)). What makes the output usable is that every prediction carries a per-residue confidence estimate, pLDDT, which the AlphaFold 2 authors present as precise per-residue estimates of the model's own reliability ([Jumper et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8371605/)). Most of the engineering in a folding pipeline is not the network but the homology search that builds the multiple sequence alignment; ColabFold replaced it with MMseqs2 for a 40 to 60 times faster search and close to 1,000 structures a day on a single GPU (Mirdita et al., [“ColabFold: making protein folding accessible to all”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9184281/) (2022)).
- **AlphaFold 3 widened the molecule types, and an open stack followed within months.** AlphaFold 3 replaces the Evoformer with a Pairformer and predicts raw atom coordinates with a diffusion module, covering complexes of proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions and modified residues (Abramson et al., [“Accurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions with AlphaFold 3”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924/) (2024)). Its access terms matter for engineers: as of November 2026 the source code is under Apache 2.0, but the weights are only available directly from Google under non-commercial terms, the AlphaFold Server covers non-commercial use with a more limited set of ligands and covalent modifications, and commercial use goes through Google Cloud ([google-deepmind/alphafold3](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold3)). Boltz-1 claims AlphaFold3-level accuracy on biomolecular complexes and was released with code, weights, datasets and benchmarks under the MIT license (Wohlwend et al., [“Boltz-1 Democratizing Biomolecular Interaction Modeling”](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.19.624167v1) (2024)), and Boltz-2 adds binding-affinity prediction that its authors say approaches free-energy perturbation accuracy at least 1,000 times more cheaply (Passaro et al., [“Boltz-2: Towards Accurate and Efficient Binding Affinity Prediction”](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707v1) (2025)). The open lineage starts earlier, with the three-track RoseTTAFold network whose accuracy approached AlphaFold 2's at CASP14 (Baek et al., [“Accurate prediction of protein structures and interactions using a 3-track neural network”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612213/) (2021)).
- **Protein language models treat sequences as text and skip the alignment step.** ESM-2 scales masked language modeling over protein sequences to 15 billion parameters, and ESMFold predicts atomic structure end to end from a single sequence, an order of magnitude faster than AlphaFold 2 (Lin et al., [“Language models of protein sequences at the scale of evolution enable accurate structure prediction”](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.20.500902v1) (2022)). That speed made bulk prediction possible: the published version reports the ESM Metagenomic Atlas, with structures for over 617 million metagenomic sequences, over 225 million of them predicted with high confidence (Lin et al., [“Evolutionary-scale prediction of atomic-level protein structure with a language model”](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade2574) (2023)). ESM3 extends the idea to a multimodal generative model over sequence, structure and function; prompted for fluorescent proteins, it produced a bright one at 58% sequence identity to the nearest known fluorescent protein (Hayes et al., [“Simulating 500 million years of evolution with a language model”](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.01.600583v1) (2024)). The title's 500 million years is an analogy, not a measurement: the authors report that natural fluorescent proteins at a similar sequence distance are separated by over five hundred million years of evolution. The largest model, at 98 billion parameters, was not released openly; a 1.4-billion-parameter version was, under the MIT license ([EvolutionaryScale, June 2024](https://www.evolutionaryscale.ai/blog/esm3-release)). The work was published in Science in January 2025 ([doi:10.1126/science.ads0018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads0018)).
- **Generative design runs the same machinery in reverse.** RFdiffusion fine-tunes RoseTTAFold on structure-denoising tasks to get a diffusion model over protein backbones, and covers unconditional monomer design, binder design, symmetric oligomers and enzyme active-site scaffolding (Watson et al., [“De novo design of protein structure and function with RFdiffusion”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10468394/) (2023)). A backbone is only a shape, so a second model, ProteinMPNN, writes the amino-acid sequence that folds into it, with 52.4% sequence recovery on native backbones against 32.9% for the physics-based Rosetta (Dauparas et al., [“Robust deep learning based protein sequence design using ProteinMPNN”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9997061/) (2022)). Designs are filtered in silico by self-consistency, folding the designed sequence with AlphaFold 2 and keeping it only if the prediction matches the design within about 2 Å and a predicted aligned error below five, and the survivors go to the bench ([Watson et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10468394/)). The denoising mathematics is the same family used for pictures, so [image generation](#image-generation) and [diffusion and flow matching](#diffusion-flow-matching) are the right background reading.
- **Genome-scale models apply the same recipe to DNA.** Evo 2 is an autoregressive DNA model at 7 and 40 billion parameters, trained on 9.3 trillion base pairs with a context window of 1 million tokens at single-nucleotide resolution using the StripedHyena 2 architecture; it predicts the functional effect of genetic variants, including clinically significant BRCA1 variants, without task-specific fine-tuning, and the parameters, training code and the OpenGenome2 dataset were all released (Brixi et al., [“Genome modeling and design across all domains of life with Evo 2”](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.18.638918v1) (2025); published in Nature in March 2026 as “Genome modelling and design across all domains of life with Evo 2”, [doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5)). AlphaGenome takes a different slice of the problem, predicting thousands of regulatory properties, such as splice junctions and chromatin accessibility, from up to 1 million DNA letters at single-base resolution ([Google DeepMind, June 2025](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphagenome-ai-for-better-understanding-the-genome/)). As of November 2026 its research code is under Apache 2.0 while the pretrained weights are downloaded from Kaggle or Hugging Face after accepting non-commercial model terms ([google-deepmind/alphagenome_research](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphagenome_research)).
- **Evaluation is where the field is honest about itself.** CASP16's monomer assessment calls single-domain fold prediction “nearly solved” but reports that progress from CASP15 was subtle and that model ranking remains a persistent weakness across most groups (Yuan et al., [“CASP16 Protein Monomer Structure Prediction Assessment”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12750037/) (2025)). For protein-ligand complexes, PoseBusters showed that low RMSD to the known binding mode is not enough and that predictions need explicit chemical and geometric validity checks, since learned methods often produce physically implausible poses (Buttenschoen et al., [“PoseBusters: AI-based docking methods fail to generate physically valid poses or generalise to novel sequences”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10901501/) (2024)). The AlphaFold 3 authors are explicit about their own limits: a 4.4% chirality violation rate on PoseBusters, occasional atom clashes, hallucinated ordered structure in disordered regions, and static predictions that do not capture conformational ensembles ([Abramson et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924/)). At the far end of the pipeline, the bar is clinical: rentosertib, a TNIK inhibitor whose target and molecule both came from a generative platform, reported a 12-week randomized phase 2a trial in 71 patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, with safety as the primary endpoint and a lung-function signal the authors say warrants larger and longer trials (Xu et al., [“A generative AI-discovered TNIK inhibitor for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a randomized phase 2a trial”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12353801/) (2025)).
- **Dual use is handled in policy and process, not inside the model alone.** In a March 2024 statement, researchers who build protein design tools committed to values and to specific practices, including obtaining “DNA synthesis services only from providers that demonstrate adherence to industry-standard biosecurity” and continuously evaluating the capabilities of their own software; as of late October 2026 the statement lists 206 signatories ([Responsible AI x Biodesign](https://responsiblebiodesign.ai/)). Screening therefore sits at the synthesis order, not only at the model. Developers of general-purpose models apply the same logic through [frontier safety frameworks](#frontier-safety-frameworks): Anthropic activated its ASL-3 deployment and security standards for Claude Opus 4 in May 2025 as a precaution against chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear misuse, combining input and output classifiers, access controls for legitimate dual-use research and weight-security measures ([Anthropic, May 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/activating-asl3-protections)), and Google DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework 3.0, published in September 2025 and updated to 3.1 in April 2026, defines critical capability levels with early-warning evaluations and safety-case reviews before launch ([Google DeepMind, September 2025](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework/)). Model builders also make data choices for the same reason: Evo 2's authors state that they excluded genomic sequences from viruses that infect eukaryotic hosts from the training data, and verified the effect by measuring the model's high perplexity on those sequences ([Brixi et al., 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.18.638918v1.full)). See [dangerous-capability evaluations](#dangerous-capability-evals) for how such thresholds are measured.

## Talk like an expert

The vocabulary: **multiple sequence alignment**, **pLDDT** and **predicted aligned error**, **co-folding**, **backbone** and **scaffold**, **binder**, **de novo design**, **inverse folding**, **self-consistency**, **in-silico success rate**, **zero-shot variant effect prediction** and **wet-lab validation**. Experts separate three very different claims about any result: it scored well on a blind benchmark, it passed an in-silico self-consistency filter, or it was made and measured in a laboratory, and they ask which one you mean before they ask anything else.
