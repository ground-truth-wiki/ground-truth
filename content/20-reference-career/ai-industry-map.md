> [!tldr]
> The AI industry is a value chain: chips and the fabs that make them, data centers and power, clouds, model developers, inference providers, and the tools and applications on top. A few large firms sit in several layers at once and are tied together by investments and compute deals, so money and dependency are concentrated. The one thing to remember: every provider you build on depends on others upstream, so treat vendor choice as a risk you manage with evals and a fallback, not a one-time decision. A dated snapshot as of November 2026.

## Key ideas

- **A value chain from silicon to applications.** The UK competition regulator describes a foundation-model value chain that runs from inputs (compute, data and expertise) through model development to deployment in apps and platforms, and finds the largest technology firms increasingly active at several levels at once ([CMA, “AI Foundation Models: Update paper” (2024)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-foundation-models-update-paper)). At the bottom, as of November 2026, sit chip designers (NVIDIA, which the CMA calls "the leading supplier of AI accelerator chips", and custom silicon such as Google's TPUs and Amazon's Trainium), a foundry layer where "a single company, TSMC, fabricates almost every leading AI chip", and data centers, of which the United States hosts 5,427, more than ten times any other country; the chips are explained in [Hardware](#hardware) and their supply chain in [Compute governance](#compute-governance) ([CMA, “AI Foundation Models: Update paper” (2024)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-foundation-models-update-paper); [Stanford HAI, 2026 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report); [Anthropic, “Expanding our use of Google Cloud TPUs and Services” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-our-use-of-google-cloud-tpus-and-services)).
- **Frontier labs have unusual structures and big backers.** OpenAI was founded as a nonprofit in December 2015, created a for-profit arm in 2019 with profits capped at 100 times any investment, and on 28 October 2025 became OpenAI Group PBC, a public benefit corporation whose board is appointed by the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation ([OpenAI (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI)). Anthropic, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI employees, is a Delaware public benefit corporation whose Long-Term Benefit Trust elects some of its board members, Amazon had invested $8 billion in it by November 2024 with AWS as its "primary cloud and training partner", and Google has invested too ([Anthropic, “The Long-Term Benefit Trust” (2023)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust); [Anthropic, “Powering the next generation of AI development with AWS” (2024)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-trainium); [Anthropic (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)). Google DeepMind formed in April 2023 by combining DeepMind, which Google acquired in 2014, with the Brain team from Google Research ([Google, “Google DeepMind: Bringing together two world-class AI teams” (2023)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/april-ai-update/); [Google DeepMind (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_DeepMind)).
- **Hyperscalers, neoclouds and brokers rent out the compute.** SemiAnalysis defines a neocloud as "a new breed of cloud compute provider focused on offering GPU compute rental", such as CoreWeave, Crusoe, Nebius and Lambda, notes that the hyperscalers' diversified businesses (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle and others) give them the lowest cost of capital, and warns that brokers and aggregators own no GPUs and that their cut "can be opaque to the end customer" ([Patel and Nishball, “AI Neocloud Playbook and Anatomy” (SemiAnalysis, 2024)](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/ai-neocloud-playbook-and-anatomy)). Labs spread their bets across chips and clouds: in October 2025 Anthropic described using Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium and NVIDIA GPUs and planned to use up to one million TPUs; see [Cloud AI platforms and inference providers](#cloud-ai-platforms) ([Anthropic, “Expanding our use of Google Cloud TPUs and Services” (2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-our-use-of-google-cloud-tpus-and-services)).
- **Five ways to sell a model.** As of November 2026, a model developer such as Anthropic sells per-token API access priced per million input and output tokens, individual subscriptions, per-seat team and enterprise plans, and access through partner-operated cloud platforms (Amazon Bedrock, and Claude models on Google Cloud when Google manages them), where the cloud provider invoices you ([Claude, “Pricing”](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Claude plans](https://claude.com/pricing)). Other developers also release open weights: Mistral "develops, or makes available, open-weight and commercial large language models"; see [Model APIs in practice](#model-apis) and [AI pricing and unit economics](#ai-pricing-unit-economics) ([Mistral, “Models Overview”](https://docs.mistral.ai/models)).
- **Money and dependency are concentrated.** The CMA found "an interconnected web of over 90 partnerships" involving Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Nvidia, and warned that partnerships could reinforce or extend existing market power ([CMA, “AI Foundation Models: Update paper” (2024)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-foundation-models-update-paper)); one layer down, CoreWeave's IPO filing shows Microsoft at 62% of its 2024 revenue and its top two customers at about 77% ([CoreWeave, Form S-1 (2025)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000119312525044231/d899798ds1.htm)), and access to compute is "a bimodal distribution": in August 2023 a handful of firms had 20,000 or more A100 or H100 GPUs, while a large "GPU-poor" group, from startups to some well-known AI firms, worked with far less ([Patel and Nishball, “Google Gemini Eats The World” (SemiAnalysis, 2023)](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/google-gemini-eats-the-world-gemini)). The 2026 AI Index reports US private AI investment of $285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4 billion, industry producing over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025, and a US-China model performance gap that has "effectively closed"; cost trends are in [Scaling trends and limits](#scaling-trends-limits) ([Stanford HAI, 2026 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)).
- **Read claims against primary data, and treat the map as a risk register.** Prefer filings and independent trackers to press coverage. Epoch AI is a "data-first research nonprofit" with databases on training compute, hardware, investment and data centers, METR is a nonprofit that studies the most advanced AI agents, EleutherAI and Ai2 are nonprofit labs that release open models, datasets and tools, and the UK AI Security Institute is a government research organization inside the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/about); [METR](https://metr.org/about); [EleutherAI](https://www.eleuther.ai/about); [Ai2](https://allenai.org/about); [AI Security Institute](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/)). For an engineer, every upstream dependency is a risk: Anthropic, for example, gives at least 60 days' notice before retiring a publicly released model, and partner clouds set their own retirement schedules, so keep your evals, a thin abstraction layer and a tested second provider (see [Gateway keys, routing and failover in depth](#gateway-routing-in-depth)) ([Claude, “Model deprecations”](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)).

## Talk like an expert

People who follow the industry talk about the value chain, hyperscalers and neoclouds, GPU-rich and GPU-poor, vertical integration, customer concentration and compute deals, and they ask of every announcement: who pays whom, for how much capacity, over what period, and where it shows up in a filing.
