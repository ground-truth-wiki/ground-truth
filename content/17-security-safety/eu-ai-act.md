> [!tldr]
> The EU AI Act, [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), regulates AI by risk: a short list of practices is banned, high-risk systems carry heavy duties for providers and deployers, some systems must disclose that they are AI, and general-purpose AI models have their own rules. It reaches companies outside the EU whose systems are sold in the EU or whose output is used there, and as of November 2026 the AI Omnibus has moved the high-risk dates to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028. Your obligations follow from your role and your use case, so settle both first; this page is information, not legal advice.

## Key ideas

- **Scope and role come first.** The Act covers providers that place AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the EU market “irrespective of whether those providers are established or located within the Union or in a third country”, and providers and deployers abroad whose system's output is used in the EU ([AI Act, Article 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)); a provider develops a system or model and places it on the market under its own name, a deployer uses one under its authority ([Article 3](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/)), and the definition of an AI system is meant to be “closely aligned with the work of international organisations working on AI” ([Recital 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/12/)); the OECD, for one, revised its definition in November 2023 ([OECD](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-publications/explanatory-memorandum-on-the-updated-oecd-definition-of-an-ai-system)). A deployer, importer or distributor becomes the provider of a high-risk system if it puts its own name or trademark on it, makes a substantial modification, or changes the intended purpose of a system, including a general-purpose one, so that it becomes high-risk ([Article 25](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/25/)).
- **Nine practices are banned, one of them from December 2026.** Eight have applied since 2 February 2025: harmful manipulation and deception, harmful exploitation of vulnerabilities, social scoring, predicting an individual's risk of committing a crime, untargeted scraping to build facial recognition databases, emotion recognition at work and in education, biometric categorisation to infer protected characteristics, and real-time remote biometric identification for law enforcement in public spaces, which has narrow exceptions ([European Commission, AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). The AI Omnibus added a ninth, on systems that generate non-consensual intimate content or child sexual abuse material, which applies from 2 December 2026 ([Future of Privacy Forum, 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)).
- **High-risk status follows Article 6, and providers carry most of the work.** A system is high-risk if it is a safety component of a product, or the product itself, that needs third-party conformity assessment under the laws in Annex I, or if it falls under an Annex III use case such as hiring, credit scoring or education; an Annex III system that only performs a narrow procedural task, improves the result of finished human work, detects deviations from earlier decision patterns without replacing human assessment, or prepares an assessment can be exempt, but never if it profiles people, and the provider must document that assessment and register the system ([Article 6](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/)). Providers of high-risk systems need risk management, data quality, logging, documentation, information for deployers, human oversight, and robustness, cybersecurity and accuracy ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)), and must report serious incidents immediately and at the latest within 15 days, within 10 days after a death and within 2 days for a widespread infringement ([Article 73](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/73/)).
- **Deployers have duties too, and transparency reaches everyone.** Before using a high-risk system, deployers that are bodies governed by public law or private entities providing public services, and those that use high-risk systems for credit scoring or life and health insurance pricing, must carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment ([Article 27](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/27/); [Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)), and people affected by significant decisions based on most Annex III systems have a right to an explanation of the system's role ([Article 86](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/86/)). Under the transparency rules of Article 50, generally applicable from 2 August 2026, people must be told when they talk to an AI system, generated content must be identifiable and deepfakes must be labeled ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)), but providers of generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to meet the marking duty of Article 50(2) ([Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj)); see [synthetic media and provenance](#synthetic-media-provenance).
- **General-purpose AI models have their own track.** Since 2 August 2025, their providers owe transparency and copyright duties, supported by scope guidelines, a voluntary Code of Practice and a template for public summaries of training content, all published in July 2025 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)); a model is presumed to carry systemic risk above 10²⁵ FLOP of training compute or if the Commission designates it ([Article 51](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/51/)), and its provider must then evaluate it, including adversarial testing, assess and mitigate systemic risks, report serious incidents and protect its cybersecurity ([Article 55](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/55/)). The Code of Practice, published 10 July 2025, has Transparency, Copyright, and Safety and Security chapters, and as of its page update of 7 October 2026 lists 22 signatories, with xAI signing only the Safety and Security chapter ([European Commission, GPAI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai)); models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply by 2 August 2027 ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)).
- **Enforcement, standards and penalties.** The AI Office and national authorities take on enforcement from 2 August 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)), harmonised standards give a presumption of conformity ([Article 40](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/40/)), and EN 18286:2026 on quality management systems became the first harmonised standard in support of the AI Act cited in the Official Journal, in October 2026 ([CEN-CENELEC](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2026/en-in-the-spotlight/2026-10-15-en-18286-2026-published-in-ojeu/)), and fines reach EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices, EUR 15 million or 3% for most other obligations and EUR 7.5 million or 1% for misleading information, whichever is higher, or lower for SMEs ([Article 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/)). The AI Omnibus, [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj), in force since 27 July 2026, also softened the AI literacy duty from ensuring it to supporting it and requires every member state to run at least one regulatory sandbox by 2 August 2027 ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)).

## Talk like an expert

Experts start with the **role** (provider, deployer, importer, distributor, downstream provider) and the **intended purpose**, then the **risk tier**, and speak of **substantial modification**, **conformity assessment**, **presumption of conformity**, **systemic risk** and the **Code of Practice**; they date every claim, because the AI Omnibus showed that the timeline can move (compare [US AI law and policy](#us-ai-law)).
