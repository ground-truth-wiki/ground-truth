> [!tldr]
> As of November 2026, the United States has no comprehensive federal AI statute: federal policy runs through executive orders that favor one light national standard and challenge state laws, and a few federal laws target specific harms. Most binding duties come from states, such as frontier-model transparency in California and New York and automated-decision rules in Colorado, California and New York City (some of these duties start only in 2027), and from regulators applying existing law. Scope your obligations by state, use case and role, and watch preemption, which could redraw this map; this page is information, not legal advice.

## Key ideas

- **Federal policy is set by executive action.** Executive Order 14110 of October 2023 was revoked on 20 January 2025 ([White House, “Initial Rescissions of Harmful Executive Orders and Actions”](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/initial-rescissions-of-harmful-executive-orders-and-actions/)), and America's AI Action Plan (July 2025) said AI-related federal funding should not go to states with burdensome AI regulations that waste it, without interfering with “prudent laws that are not unduly restrictive to innovation” ([America's AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf)). Executive Order 14365 of 11 December 2025 ordered a Justice Department AI Litigation Task Force to challenge conflicting state laws, a Commerce review of onerous state laws, limits on BEAD non-deployment funds for those states, an FCC proceeding on a preemptive federal reporting and disclosure standard, an FTC policy statement on deceptive practices, and a legislative recommendation that spares state child-safety, compute infrastructure and procurement laws ([Executive Order 14365](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/)).
- **Preemption is a proposal to Congress, not law.** The White House recommendations of March 2026 ask Congress to preempt state AI laws that “impose undue burdens” and to bar states from regulating AI development, while keeping state powers over generally applicable laws such as child protection, the zoning of AI infrastructure and a state's own use of AI, and they reject any new federal rulemaking body for AI ([White House, “National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence: Legislative Recommendations”](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf)). If Congress acts or the Justice Department's challenges succeed, the state laws below could narrow or fall, so date every compliance decision.
- **Federal laws and agencies target specific harms.** The TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed in May 2025, requires platforms to remove non-consensual intimate images within 48 hours of a valid request, and the FTC began enforcing that duty on 19 May 2026, with civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation ([FTC, May 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act)). NIST's center for frontier AI testing, called CAISI in the Action Plan, is named on NIST's page as of November 2026 the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), which leads unclassified evaluations of national security risks such as cybersecurity, biosecurity and chemical weapons ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/caisi)), and an executive order of 29 September 2026 told the executive branch to replace the term “Artificial Intelligence” with “Super Intelligence” in its policy and other non-statutory documents ([White House fact sheet, September 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-inaugurates-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)).
- **California and New York regulate the largest frontier developers.** California's Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (SB 53), signed 29 September 2025, requires large frontier developers to publish a frontier AI framework, protects whistleblowers, lets companies and the public report critical safety incidents to the Office of Emergency Services, and creates the CalCompute consortium ([Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/)); it has applied since 1 January 2026, with incident reports due within 15 days, or 24 hours for an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury ([Cooley, April 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-31-new-yorks-frontier-ai-law-gets-a-california-makeover-with-some-key-differences)). New York's RAISE Act, as amended by chapter 96 of 27 March 2026, applies from 1 January 2027, with 72-hour incident reports to an office within the Department of Financial Services and penalties of up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million after that, and both laws cover models trained with more than 10²⁶ operations and add duties for developers with more than $500 million in annual revenue ([New York Senate, S8828](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8828); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-31-new-yorks-frontier-ai-law-gets-a-california-makeover-with-some-key-differences)).
- **Automated-decision laws cover employment, housing, financial services and more.** Colorado's SB 26-189, signed 14 May 2026, repeals and replaces the 2024 Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) and, from 1 January 2027, requires deployers of automated decision-making technology that materially influences a consequential decision to give notice, explain adverse outcomes within 30 days, let consumers correct their data and offer meaningful human review on request, to the extent commercially reasonable, enforced only by the Attorney General ([Norton Rose Fulbright, 2026](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/18733d31/colorado-enacts-revised-ai-law)). California's CCPA regulations on automated decision-making technology, risk assessments and cybersecurity audits took effect on 1 January 2026, with ADMT duties from 1 January 2027 and audit certifications phased from April 2028 to April 2030 by revenue ([California Privacy Protection Agency, September 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html)), and New York City's Local Law 144, enforced since 5 July 2023, requires that an automated employment decision tool has had a bias audit within one year of its use, a public summary of the results and notice to candidates ([NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page)).
- **Chatbot, child-safety and sector rules add more.** California's SB 243 on companion chatbots, signed 13 October 2025 and in force since 1 January 2026, requires operators to keep a protocol for suicidal ideation and self-harm that refers users to crisis services, to remind minors that they are talking to AI and keep sexual material from them, and to report annually, and it gives families a private right of action ([Senator Padilla, October 2025](https://sd18.senate.ca.gov/news/first-nation-ai-chatbot-safeguards-signed-law); [Governor of California, October 2025](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/10/13/governor-newsom-signs-bills-to-further-strengthen-californias-leadership-in-protecting-children-online/)). Existing anti-discrimination rules also reach AI tools: under federal employment guidelines, a group's selection rate below four-fifths of the highest group's rate is generally treated as evidence of adverse impact ([29 CFR 1607.4(D)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1607.4)); see [fairness metrics and bias measurement](#fairness-bias).

## Talk like an expert

Experts scope by **jurisdiction**, **use case** (frontier development, **consequential decisions**, hiring, companion chatbots) and **role** (developer or deployer), speak of **large frontier developers**, **critical safety incidents**, **covered ADMT**, **adverse impact** and **bias audits**, and check first whether **preemption** litigation or legislation has moved (compare [the EU AI Act](#eu-ai-act)).
