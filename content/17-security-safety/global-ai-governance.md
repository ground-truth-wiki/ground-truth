> [!tldr]
> Outside the EU and the US, AI rules range from China's binding rules for each kind of AI service, through South Korea's AI Basic Act and Japan's promotion law, to the UK's reliance on existing regulators, with non-binding principles, summits and a shared scientific report on top. For a product offered in many markets, the work is a dated map of which rules each market triggers, confirmed by counsel: this page is a snapshot as of November 2026 and information, not legal advice.

## Key ideas

- **China regulates AI one service type at a time.** Algorithm recommendation rules (effective March 2022) brought algorithm filing with the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), followed by deep synthesis rules (January 2023) and the Interim Measures for generative AI services (in force 15 August 2023) ([Wikipedia, “Regulation of artificial intelligence”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence)). As of November 2026 the stack also includes labeling measures in force since 1 September 2025, with visible labels on AI-generated content and implicit labels in file metadata ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)), an amended Cybersecurity Law whose new Article 20 covers support for AI and its risk supervision, in force 1 January 2026 ([China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-cybersecurity-law-amendment/)), and measures for anthropomorphic AI services built on sustained emotional interaction, in force since 15 July 2026 ([Science and Technology Daily](https://stdaily.com/web/English/2026-07/28/content_554571.html)).
- **The UK leaves AI to its existing regulators.** The white paper “A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation” (29 March 2023) set general principles for existing regulators to apply in their own sectors, and the government chose not to legislate early ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence)), and the AI Safety Institute became the AI Security Institute on 14 February 2025, focused on serious risks with security implications ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tackling-ai-security-risks-to-unleash-growth-and-deliver-plan-for-change)). As of September 2026 the UK still had no comprehensive AI law, and the expected AI bill had not appeared ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/14/ai-judgment-day-on-the-horizon-while-uk-lawmakers-play-catch-up)).
- **South Korea and Japan passed framework laws with very different teeth.** South Korea's AI Basic Act, passed on 26 December 2024 ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/01/south-korea-national-assembly-passes-new-ai-bill/)), took effect with its Enforcement Decree on 22 January 2026: it sets duties for high-impact AI (such as healthcare, energy, transport, hiring and biometrics), requires notice and labeling for generative AI, adds safety duties for models trained with 10²⁶ FLOP or more, and allows fines of up to KRW 30 million, deferred during a grace period of at least a year ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); [US International Trade Administration](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)). Japan's AI Promotion Act, passed on 28 May 2025 and mostly in force from 4 June 2025, creates an AI Strategic Headquarters chaired by the Prime Minister and an AI Basic Plan, and contains no explicit penalties ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-japans-ai-promotion-act-an-innovation-first-blueprint-for-ai-regulation/); [Japanese Law Translation](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/outlines/view/168)).
- **International instruments are mostly soft law.** The OECD AI Principles (adopted 2019, updated May 2024, 47 adherents as of November 2026) define an AI system as “a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers” how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions ([OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles)), and the G7 Hiroshima Process code of conduct (30 October 2023) gives voluntary guidance to developers of the most advanced systems ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/hiroshima-process-international-code-conduct-advanced-ai-systems)). The Council of Europe's Framework Convention, a treaty, opened for signature on 5 September 2024, and UN resolution A/RES/79/325 (26 August 2025) created an Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and a Global Dialogue on AI Governance ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence); [United Nations](https://www.un.org/global-digital-compact/en/ai)).
- **The summit series produced declarations, commitments and a report, not law.** Bletchley (November 2023) produced the Bletchley Declaration of 28 countries and the EU ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023)); Seoul (2024) the Frontier AI Safety Commitments, in which 16 organizations promised to publish safety frameworks with risk thresholds ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024)); Paris (February 2025) a statement on inclusive and sustainable AI that the US and the UK did not sign ([Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2025/02/11/statement-on-inclusive-and-sustainable-artificial-intelligence-for-people-and-the-planet)); and New Delhi (February 2026) the New Delhi Declaration, endorsed by 88 countries and international organizations ([All India Radio](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/india-ai-impact-summit-2026-adopts-new-delhi-declaration)). The International AI Safety Report, chaired by Yoshua Bengio, followed its January 2025 edition with a second on 3 February 2026, written by over 100 experts ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).
- **Scope a product by market, role and feature.** National laws reach providers abroad: South Korea's act applies to AI systems operated from abroad that affect users or markets in Korea, and foreign companies above revenue or user thresholds (such as one million average daily Korean users) must appoint a local representative ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)). Keep a dated register for each market of the duties your role and features trigger (labels, user notices, filings, representatives, compute thresholds), and have counsel confirm it.

## Talk like an expert

Experts separate **binding law** from **soft law** (principles, codes of conduct, declarations), then ask what triggers each rule: where users are, which role you play (provider, deployer, platform) and which kind of service you run. They read summit outputs as signals of direction, not as obligations, and date every claim.
